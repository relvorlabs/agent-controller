# The 15 Ways Pitch Decks Kill Deals

> Most decks don't fail because the business is bad.
> They fail because the deck fails to communicate why the business is good.

---

## Failure 1: Leading With the Product, Not the Problem
The audience hasn't felt the pain yet. Showing your solution before they feel the pain is like offering medicine before they're sick. **Fix:** Spend slides 2–3 making them feel the problem viscerally before you name your product.

---

## Failure 2: Headlines That Are Labels, Not Insights
"Market Size" tells me nothing. "A $47B market where the dominant player has 2% market share" tells me everything. **Fix:** Every headline must contain a fact, a number, or a surprise. Labels are wasted slide real estate.

---

## Failure 3: The Realistic TAM That Is Actually Nothing
"Our target market is 8,000 property agencies in Lagos." With no revenue context, that sounds small. **Fix:** Anchor the TAM to a revenue outcome: "8,000 agencies at ₦80K/month = ₦7.7B/year. We need 150 to hit $1M ARR."

---

## Failure 4: No "Why Now"
Ideas don't exist in time-neutral space. What changed that makes this possible now? **Fix:** Add one slide or section that names the specific thing that changed (regulation, technology cost, behaviour shift) that opens the window.

---

## Failure 5: Traction Hidden or Soft-Pedalled
Founders are often embarrassed by small traction. Investors are not. They know how early you are. But they want to see *some* signal. **Fix:** Name every true signal — even 3 customer conversations, 1 paid pilot, 47 waitlist signups.

---

## Failure 6: The Team Slide Is a LinkedIn Profile
A list of names and titles tells an investor nothing. **Fix:** The team slide answers: "Why are these specific people uniquely positioned to win this specific market?" One paragraph per founder. Background + market connection + unfair advantage.

---

## Failure 7: The Ask Is Vague
"We are raising a seed round" is not an ask. **Fix:** State the amount, the instrument (SAFE, equity, debt), the use of funds in 3 lines, and the milestone it funds.

---

## Failure 8: Ignoring Competition
"We have no competitors" signals naivety. Everything competes with something. Even Excel and WhatsApp are competitors. **Fix:** Show the competitive landscape honestly. Position against it with a specific, defensible differentiation.

---

## Failure 9: Projections That Explode Into Unrealism
Year 3 projections that reach $50M when Year 1 is $0 with no model to bridge them. **Fix:** If you include projections, show the unit economics that justify them. Or don't include projections — instead show the assumptions and let the investor calculate.

---

## Failure 10: Passive Language About the Problem
"There is a lack of transparency in the market." That's passive. It doesn't make anyone feel anything. **Fix:** Name a person and give them a moment. "Adeola manages 200 units across Lagos. Every month, she spends 3 days chasing rent via WhatsApp. She has never once been able to tell a landlord the true state of arrears in under an hour."

---

## Failure 11: The Solution Slide That Lists Features
"Our platform provides: receipt management, payment tracking, tenant history, and reporting." This is a feature list. It creates zero desire. **Fix:** The solution slide shows the outcome, not the feature. "Leja turns every payment into a tamper-evident record that follows the tenant forever — and gives the agent an intelligence dashboard that shows exactly who has paid, who is at risk, and who is about to default."

---

## Failure 12: No Emotional Arc
Some decks are entirely rational: here is the problem, here is the data, here is the solution, here is the ask. Correct but cold. **Fix:** One story — real or composite — of a real person experiencing the problem and the relief. Emotion creates memory. Memory creates follow-up.

---

## Failure 13: Too Many Slides
20+ slide decks signal that the founder cannot prioritise. Investors have 47 other decks. **Fix:** 10–13 slides maximum for investor deck. Cut everything that does not answer a question the investor is already asking.

---

## Failure 14: Jargon That Isn't Shared
"AI-native trust infrastructure" is clear to you and opaque to a generalist investor. **Fix:** Jargon must be earned. Define it once, immediately, in plain English. Then use it.

---

## Failure 15: The Last Slide Is "Thank You"
A thank-you slide closes the energy of the room. **Fix:** The last slide in the room is the ask. The last slide on the deck sent via email is contact information + the ask, together.

---

## Bonus: The Pre-Meeting Failure
Many deals die before the meeting because the intro email was weak.

The intro email must do one thing: make the investor curious enough to open the deck.

**Weak:** "Hi, I'm Surdma. I'm building Leja, a property management platform in Nigeria. Would love to share our deck."

**Strong:** "Lagos agents manage 50–500 units each with no institutional record of who has paid, who hasn't, or who is a repeat defaulter. Leja is the verified ledger that fixes this — and builds the buyer-side credit layer as a structural byproduct. 3 agencies, 412 units, ₦18M tracked in 6 weeks. Deck attached."
