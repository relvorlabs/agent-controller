# Deck Types: Full Guidance

---

## Investor Deck (Seed / Pre-Seed)

**Audience mindset:** Skeptical by default. They've seen 1,000 decks. They're looking for a reason to say no, not yes.
**What converts them:** Conviction that the founder has insight others don't + evidence that someone cares.
**Biggest mistake:** Explaining the product before establishing the problem.
**Tone:** Confident, precise, honest. Never oversell. Investors are lie detectors.

**Required elements:**
- The problem quantified (not described)
- The insight (what do you see that others don't?)
- Why now (what changed?)
- Evidence of demand (even tiny)
- A clear, specific ask

---

## Investor Deck (Series A)

**Audience mindset:** Looking for proof, not promise. They're pattern-matching against what they've seen scale.
**What converts them:** Metrics that compound + a repeatable acquisition motion.
**Biggest mistake:** Still selling the vision when you should be selling the machine.
**Tone:** Data-heavy, precise. Lead with metrics, follow with story.

**Required elements:**
- MRR / ARR with growth rate
- Unit economics (LTV, CAC, payback period)
- Net retention rate
- Go-to-market repeatability evidence
- Product roadmap tied to business metrics, not feature wishes

## Sales / Customer Deck

**Audience mindset:** Skeptical of vendors. They've been sold to many times and bought things that didn't work.
**What converts them:** Feeling understood before being sold to. Proof someone like them got a result.
**Biggest mistake:** Leading with your company or product. They don't care about you. They care about their problem.
**Tone:** Consultative. You're a peer who solved their problem, not a vendor pitching.

**Required elements:**
- Mirror their exact situation in your language (not your product's language)
- Quantify the cost of their status quo
- A specific case study with real numbers
- A low-friction next step (not "buy now" but "30-minute call this week")

---

## Partnership / Strategic Deck

**Audience mindset:** Risk-averse. They want wins without risk to their existing business.
**What converts them:** Clear mutual gain + minimum friction for them.
**Biggest mistake:** Making it about what you need from them.
**Tone:** Collaborative. "We" language, not "I" language.

**Required elements:**
- What they get (specific and first)
- What you get (honest and second)
- Why you specifically (not just any partner)
- A clear, small first step

---

## Demo Day Deck

**Audience mindset:** Scanning quickly. They will hear 15 pitches. They remember 2.
**What converts them:** One memorable insight or number that sticks.
**Biggest mistake:** Trying to explain everything. Less is more.
**Tone:** High energy. Simple. Memorable.

**Required elements:**
- One number that lands
- One line that is quotable
- A face or a moment (story)
- The ask in 10 words

**Special rule for demo day:** Your deck is a trailer, not a film. Every slide must make them want to know more — not answer every question.

---

## Accelerator / Grant Application Deck

**Audience mindset:** Looking for potential that fits their thesis + founders they believe in.
**What converts them:** Demonstrated hunger and self-awareness about what you need to learn.
**Biggest mistake:** Pretending you've figured everything out. Accelerators invest in learning velocity.
**Tone:** Honest, curious, coachable — while still demonstrating conviction.

**Required elements:**
- Why you fit their thesis specifically
- What you will learn/prove in their program
- What you're building and why it matters
- Who you are and why you're the right founder for this

---

## Internal / Board Deck

**Audience mindset:** They are not being asked to invest. They are being asked to approve, allocate, or align.
**What converts them:** Clarity about what you're asking for and why it's worth the risk.
**Biggest mistake:** Using investor-pitch framing in an internal context. Internal stakeholders want specificity, not vision.
**Tone:** Direct, analytical, balanced (acknowledge tradeoffs explicitly).

**Required elements:**
- Clear recommendation
- The decision you need made
- Options considered (show you thought it through)
- Tradeoffs honestly stated
- Resource requirements and timeline
