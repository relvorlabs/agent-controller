# How Investors Actually Make Decisions

> What investors say they care about and what actually moves them are different things.
> Build for the actual, not the stated.

---

## The 60-Second Filter

Every investor filters decks in the first 60 seconds. During those 60 seconds, they are asking exactly three questions:

1. **Do I understand what this is?** (If not, it's dead)
2. **Is this market big enough to matter?** (If not, it's dead)
3. **Does this founder seem credible?** (If not, it's dead — no matter how good the product)

Your first two slides must pass all three filters. The rest of the deck builds the case for someone who passed the filter.

## What Early-Stage Investors Actually Bet On

Ranked by actual weight in decision (not stated weight):

| Factor | What They Say | What They Mean |
|---|---|---|
| Founder | "We invest in founders" | Does this person have an unusual insight and can they sell/execute? |
| Market | "We need a large market" | Is there a path to $100M+ revenue? |
| Traction | "Show us product-market fit" | Is there any evidence real people want this? |
| Product | "We love the product" | Do I believe the technical approach is defensible? |
| Model | "Clear path to revenue" | Does the math hold under stress? |
| Team | "Full team is important" | Does the founder know what they don't know? |

## The Founder-Market Fit Obsession

Before Series A, investors are primarily backing the founder.

The unspoken question is: **Why will this specific person succeed where others have failed or not tried?**

The answer must be one or more of:
- **Unique insight** — They discovered something non-obvious and true
- **Unique access** — They have distribution, relationships, or domain others cannot replicate
- **Unique conviction** — They have personal stakes that ensure they won't quit
- **Unique timing** — They entered the problem at the precise moment the environment changed

Your team slide is not a résumé. It is a "why us and only us" argument.

## The TAM Trap

Most founders present TAM numbers that investors immediately discount.

**The wrong way:**
"The Nigerian real estate market is $17.9B."

This means nothing to an investor. It's the whole ocean.

**The right way:**
Build the market from the bottom up:
- "There are ~35,000 licensed property agents in Nigeria"
- "Of those, ~8,000 manage 20+ units and have operational pain worth paying to solve"
- "At ₦80,000/month average, that's a ₦7.7B/year serviceable market — and we only need 0.5% to hit $1M ARR"

Bottom-up TAM shows you understand your customer unit. Top-down TAM just shows you can Google.

## The Traction Spectrum

Zero traction does not disqualify an early-stage deck. But you must understand where you are and be honest about it.

| Stage | What Counts as Traction | How to Frame It |
|---|---|---|
| Idea stage | Problem interviews, waitlist signups | "Validated the pain with 47 conversations" |
| Design partner | Letters of intent, pilot agreements | "3 agencies committed to test in Month 1" |
| Revenue | Paying customers, even small | "₦180K MRR from 4 customers in 6 weeks" |
| Growth | MoM growth rate | "Growing 40% month-on-month for 3 months" |
| Scale | NRR, LTV, CAC ratios | [Series A territory] |

**Never pretend you're higher on the spectrum than you are.** Investors will find out. Trust, once broken, ends the deal.

## The Questions They Ask vs. The Questions They Mean

| Asked | Meant |
|---|---|
| "How did you arrive at this market size?" | "Are you delusional or grounded?" |
| "What's your go-to-market?" | "Do you know how to find and close a customer without burning cash?" |
| "Who else is doing this?" | "Do you know your competitive landscape, and is your differentiation real?" |
| "What does your team look like?" | "Can you execute without me babysitting you?" |
| "What will you do with the money?" | "Is this founder capital-efficient or a burner?" |
| "What keeps you up at night?" | "Is this founder self-aware about risk?" |

Prepare honest, specific answers to the "meant" question, not the "asked" question.

## The Valuation / Ask Slide

Most founders bury or soft-pedal the ask. This is a mistake. Investors respect clarity.

**State clearly:**
- How much you are raising: exact number
- What you are offering: equity percentage or SAFE/convertible terms
- What milestone this funds: the specific outcome you will achieve
- By when: the timeline

**Example (strong):**
> "We are raising $350,000 on a SAFE at a $2.5M cap. This funds 18 months of runway to hit ₦10M MRR and 500 active agencies — the threshold for launching Leja Score and raising a Series A on data, not theory."

**Example (weak):**
> "We are currently raising a seed round and would love to talk about terms."

Weak asks signal a founder who is not ready.

## What Kills Deals in Q&A

These answers end conversations:
- "We don't have competitors" (shows naivety)
- "Our growth will be organic" (shows no GTM thinking)
- "We'll figure out monetization later" (shows no business model discipline)
- "The technology is very complex" (explained defensively — suggests it doesn't work)
- "Our market is everyone" (shows no customer focus)
- Defensive body language or dismissing an objection as wrong

The correct response to any hard Q&A question: "That's the right question. Here's how we think about it: [honest, specific answer]."
