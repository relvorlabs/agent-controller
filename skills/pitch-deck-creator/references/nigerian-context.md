# Nigerian Startup & PropTech Pitch Context

> Pitching in the Nigerian ecosystem requires understanding its specific investor landscape,
> its distinct proof standards, and how global frameworks must be localised.

---

## The Nigerian Investor Landscape

### Local ecosystem
- **Angel networks**: Lagos Angel Network, VC4A Africa, individual HNWIs (high net worth individuals)
- **Local VCs**: Ventures Platform, Founders Factory Africa, Voltron Capital, Future Africa, Ingressive Capital
- **Accelerators**: YC (African cohorts growing), Techstars, Google for Startups, MEST Africa
- **DFIs**: IFC, British International Investment (BII), AfricaInvestor, Afreximbank

### What Nigerian/African investors specifically care about
1. **Real problem, not imported problem** — Is this a genuine African problem or a transplanted Silicon Valley solution?
2. **Currency risk awareness** — Have you modelled FX exposure and inflation into your unit economics?
3. **Regulatory navigation** — Do you understand CBN, LASRERA, SEC, NDPC, FIRS exposure?
4. **Infrastructure resilience** — Does your product work when NEPA (power) fails, JAMB crashes, or internet is throttled?
5. **Founder local credibility** — Do you have the network to sell in Nigeria specifically?

## What Makes Nigeria Context Different in a Pitch

### The trust problem is the pitch
In Nigeria, institutional memory is a genuine gap. This is the unusual starting point. You can lead with it without explaining it — sophisticated Nigerian investors already know the problem. What they need to believe is:
- Your approach to closing this gap is credible
- You have a path to monetisation that doesn't require volume before revenue
- You understand the informal infrastructure (agents, WhatsApp groups, NIESV chapters) that you must work through

### The compliance wedge
Nigerian B2B products that cite regulatory compliance unlock buying authority faster than feature lists. If your product helps a business comply with existing law (Lagos Tenancy Law, NDPC, FIRS receipting rules), lead with that in sales pitches. Compliance removes an obstacle that the customer already knows is a problem.

### The mobile-first assumption
Any product that assumes desktop-first is pitching against the grain. Show mobile UX. Show offline resilience. Show USSD or WhatsApp fallback if relevant.

### Cash-first economy
A significant portion of rent in Nigeria is collected in cash. If your product handles or integrates with cash flow, say so explicitly. "We capture the cash layer" is a product insight, not a limitation.

## Nigerian PropTech Landscape (Leja Context)

### Existing players and how to position against them
- **PropertyPro / Propertybase** — Listings portals. Not operational tools. Not trust infrastructure.
- **Spleet / Kwaba** — Rent financing. They need tenancy records Leja generates. Partnership potential > competition.
- **Paystack / Flutterwave** — Payment rails. Not property-specific. Cannot produce tamper-evident tenancy records.
- **Landwey / Northcourt** — Developer-focused. Not agent ops.
- **No direct competitor** currently occupies the verified tenancy ledger + AI ops layer space

### The "why is this not built yet" objection
Common in Nigerian pitches. Strong answer:
> "Because Lagos agents are 90–100% informal, and fintech companies built payment infrastructure, not trust infrastructure. The gap exists because it sits at the intersection of legal compliance, agent operations, and data intelligence — three domains that have never been bundled. We are building at that intersection."

## Proof Standards for Nigerian Pitches

**What counts as validation in the Nigerian context:**
- Customer conversations: minimum 20, quoted specifically
- WhatsApp group activity: a community of interested users
- A live pilot, even unpaid or manual
- A letter of intent from a Lagos agency
- Early revenue: even ₦10,000 in subscription fees is a signal

**What does NOT count:**
- "We surveyed 100 people on WhatsApp and they said they'd pay"
- "Google says the market is $17.9B"
- "There's nothing like this in Nigeria"

Nigerian investors — especially those who have seen the ecosystem closely — will press harder on validation than Silicon Valley investors because they know how many Nigerian products have launched to silence.

## Language to Use / Avoid in Nigerian Startup Pitches

### Use
- Specific ₦ amounts (shows market knowledge)
- Regulatory reference by name (Lagos Tenancy Law, NDPC, FIRS e-receipt)
- "Units under management" instead of "users" for a property tool
- "Design partners" for pre-revenue relationships
- "Trust layer" / "verified record" / "institutional memory" for the product category

### Avoid
- "Disrupting" (cliché, now signals naivety)
- "Uber for X" or "Airbnb for X" (lazy framing)
- "We will educate the market" (code for: no one wants this yet)
- "We plan to monetise later" (capital inefficiency signal)
- "Nigeria is a $X trillion economy" (generic filler)

## The Relvor / Leja Pitch Specifics

**The core pitch positioning (non-negotiable):**
> Leja is Nigeria's first AI-native property trust infrastructure — a verified ledger that gives agents a source of truth about their buyers, gives buyers portable credibility that follows them forever, and uses AI as the intelligence layer that makes every interaction smarter, every pattern visible, and every decision data-driven.

**The emotional core:**
In a country where every real estate deal starts from zero, Leja creates institutional memory. Trust, proof, permanence — where before there was only WhatsApp screenshots.

**The investor hook:**
The seller product generates transaction data. The data trains the AI. The AI creates the score. The score becomes the infrastructure. Infrastructure becomes an API that other products consume. This is the compounding flywheel that makes Leja defensible — not just useful.

**The "why now" answer for Leja:**
- NDPC (data protection) enforcement is increasing
- Lagos Tenancy Law non-compliance is becoming a liability
- AI infrastructure costs have collapsed (intelligence that cost $500K/year in 2020 is now $500/month)
- Lagos rental market is growing — more transactions means more pain, more urgency, more willingness to pay for a solution

**The most important thing to convey in any Leja pitch:**
The data flywheel. Agents come for compliance receipts. The side effect is the scoring system. The scoring system is the moat. Without this insight, Leja sounds like a receipt tool. With it, Leja sounds like infrastructure.
