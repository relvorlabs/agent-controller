# Pitch Deck Creator

> A deck does not inform. It converts.
> The test is not "do they understand?" The test is "do they act?"

---

## Before You Write a Single Slide

Read `references/psychology.md` first. Understanding *why* pitches succeed and fail is what separates a converting deck from a beautiful failure. This skill is worthless without that foundation.

Then come back here and follow the build process.

---

## Step 1: Interrogate Before You Build

Do NOT start writing slides. Start by extracting the minimum viable pitch context.

Ask the user for:

1. **Who are you pitching to, specifically?** (VC firm, angel, bank, customer, partner, government, internal board)
2. **What do they care about most?** (ROI, market size, risk reduction, innovation, social proof, compliance)
3. **What do you want them to do after?** (Wire money, sign contract, take a meeting, approve budget, refer someone)
4. **What is the single hardest objection they will raise?** (The one that keeps you up at night)
5. **What is your current traction?** (Be brutally honest — zero is fine if you're honest about why it's about to change)
6. **What is your unfair advantage?** (The thing you have that others cannot easily copy)

If the user says "I don't know" to any of these, that is the real problem. Work with them on those answers before touching slide structure.

## Step 2: Diagnose the Deck Type

See `references/deck-types.md` for full guidance on each.

| Type | Audience | Primary Goal | Dominant Emotion to Create |
|---|---|---|---|
| Investor Seed/Pre-Seed | Angels, early VCs | Believe in founder + market | Excitement + FOMO |
| Investor Series A+ | Institutional VCs | Believe in metrics + moat | Conviction + urgency |
| Customer / Sales | Decision-maker at a company | Buy or sign | Fear of staying behind + relief |
| Partnership | Strategic partners | Collaborate | Mutual gain + trust |
| Internal | Board, leadership | Approve budget/direction | Confidence + clarity |
| Accelerator / Grant | Programs, government | Select for program | Potential + fit |
| Demo Day | Mixed room | Interest + follow-up conversations | Memorable first impression |

## Step 3: Choose the Narrative Engine

Every winning pitch uses one of three narrative engines. Choosing wrong is fatal.

### Engine A: The Problem-First Narrative (for markets where the pain is visible)
```
World as it is → Cost of that world → Our fix → Why we can fix it → Proof → Future world → Join us
```
Use for: B2B software, infrastructure, ops tools, compliance tools

### Engine B: The Insight-First Narrative (for counter-intuitive ideas)
```
Unpopular truth nobody says out loud → Why it's been ignored → What it actually means → We built for it → Evidence → The edge this creates → Join us
```
Use for: contrarian bets, paradigm shifts, markets where incumbents "have it backwards"

### Engine C: The Traction-First Narrative (for post-revenue companies)
```
The numbers first → What's behind the numbers → Why they grow → What happens when we scale → Join us
```
Use for: Series A+, later-stage pitches, any deck where the metrics are the story

**Default to Engine A** unless you have a genuinely contrarian insight (Engine B) or strong revenue metrics (Engine C).

## Step 4: Build the Deck Structure

### Investor Deck (Seed / Pre-Seed) — 10–13 slides

Read `references/investor-psychology.md` before writing this type.

| # | Slide | Purpose | Key Question It Must Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | First impression | What do you do? (under 8 words) |
| 2 | The Problem | Create pain recognition | Do I feel this? Is this real? |
| 3 | The Insight | Intellectual hook | Is this obvious in hindsight? Smart? |
| 4 | The Solution | Relief | Does this solve the pain I just felt? |
| 5 | Why Now | Urgency | Why is this the right moment? |
| 6 | Market | Size the prize | Is this worth my time? |
| 7 | Product | Trust | Does it actually work? |
| 8 | Business Model | Return path | How does this become money? |
| 9 | Traction | De-risk | Is there any evidence? |
| 10 | Go-to-Market | Credibility | Do they know how to sell? |
| 11 | Team | Conviction | Can they actually execute? |
| 12 | The Ask | Action | What am I being asked to do? |
| 13 | Appendix | Defense | (Supporting data — not for main flow) |

### Customer / Sales Deck — 8–10 slides

| # | Slide | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | Frame it around *their* world, not yours |
| 2 | Their World Right Now | Mirror their current pain — make them nod |
| 3 | Cost of Inaction | Quantify what staying still costs them |
| 4 | What a Better World Looks Like | Paint the outcome before you name the solution |
| 5 | Our Solution | Now introduce yourself |
| 6 | How It Works | Enough detail to believe, not enough to overwhelm |
| 7 | Evidence | Case study, metrics, or social proof |
| 8 | What We Need From You | Specific next step |
| 9 | FAQs / Objection Pre-empts | (optional) |

## Step 5: Write Each Slide

For every slide, deliver all five elements:

```
### Slide [N]: [Slide Name]

**Headline (what appears on screen — the one thing they must remember):**
[One sentence. Subject + verb + specific number or insight. Never a label.]

**Visual direction:**
[Specific visual — not "an image" but exactly what kind and what it should show]

**Body copy (max 30 words total — 3–5 bullets):**
- [Most important point]
- [Second point]
- [Third point]

**Speaker script (what you say, not what's on screen):**
[2–4 sentences. Conversational. Must create the target emotion for this slide.]

**Psychological objective:**
[What belief or feeling must the audience hold after this slide?]
```

---

## Step 6: Headline Rules (Non-Negotiable)

Headlines are the only thing most people remember from a deck. If your headline is a label, you wasted the slide.

**The Test:** Read only the headlines in sequence. Do they tell a complete story? Do they build to a conclusion? If yes: strong deck. If no: rework.

| BAD (labels — tell nothing) | GOOD (insights — tell everything) |
|---|---|
| "The Problem" | "Lagos landlords lose ₦2.3M/year to untracked rent arrears" |
| "Market Size" | "68 million renters in Nigeria — zero have a portable payment record" |
| "Our Solution" | "Leja turns every rent payment into a tamper-proof credibility record" |
| "Traction" | "3 agencies, 412 units, ₦18M tracked in 6 weeks" |
| "Team" | "The only team in Nigeria building at the intersection of real estate ops and AI trust infrastructure" |
| "The Ask" | "We are raising $400K to hit 500 active agencies before June 2026" |

## Step 7: The Objection Inoculation Technique

Every investor or customer has 3–5 silent objections they will not say out loud. If you don't address them, the deck fails *silently*.

List the top objections for this audience. Then pre-empt them:

**Types of silent objections:**
- **Why you?** (founder-market fit doubts)
- **Why now?** (timing skepticism)
- **Is the market real?** (size concerns)
- **Can this scale?** (unit economics doubt)
- **What if X competitor does this?** (competitive threat)
- **Is the data real?** (traction credibility)

Inoculate by *naming the objection before the investor does*, then answering it on your terms.

Example: Instead of ignoring the "but there's no data moat yet" objection:
> "We know what you're thinking — how do you build a trust layer without initial data? The answer is the reverse flywheel: agents onboard for the compliance receipts alone. Every payment creates the data. After 500 units, the pattern recognition begins."

---

## Step 8: The Closing Slide (Most Abused Slide in Existence)

The final slide is not a "thank you." It is the conversion engine.

**It must contain:**
1. The ask — stated with precision (amount, equity, timeline, or action)
2. What the money/commitment unlocks — the milestone they are funding
3. The return signal — why this is the moment to act (FOMO, traction, timing)
4. Contact — single email, no noise

**It must not contain:**
- Generic "Thank You"
- A logo without context
- A question mark

---

## Step 9: The Deck Review Checklist

Before finalising, run every slide through this:

**Story test:**
- [ ] Reading headlines only tells a complete story
- [ ] Every slide adds to the narrative — none are filler
- [ ] The ask is specific (not "we're raising a round" but "we're raising $X at $Y valuation")

**Slide test (per slide):**
- [ ] One idea per slide — only one
- [ ] Headline is an insight, not a label
- [ ] Body under 30 words
- [ ] Visual is specific, not generic
- [ ] Speaker notes exist and are conversational

**Audience test:**
- [ ] Every objection they will raise is addressed somewhere
- [ ] The deck uses their language, not your jargon
- [ ] The emotional arc lands: pain → hope → conviction → action

**Honesty test:**
- [ ] No claim that cannot be sourced or defended
- [ ] Traction is presented accurately (not inflated, not undersold)
- [ ] Risks acknowledged, at least implicitly

## Step 10: Format for Output

```
# Pitch Deck: [Name]
**Deck type:** [Investor / Sales / Partnership / etc.]
**Narrative engine:** [A / B / C]
**Target audience:** [Specific]
**The ask:** [One line]
**Primary emotion to create:** [One word or phrase]

---

### Slide 1: [Name]
**Headline:** ...
**Visual:** ...
**Body:**
- ...
**Speaker script:** ...
**Psychological objective:** ...

[Repeat for all slides]

---

## Objection Pre-empts
[List 3–5 objections with counter-framing]

## What Not to Say
[Common mistakes for this specific audience]
```
